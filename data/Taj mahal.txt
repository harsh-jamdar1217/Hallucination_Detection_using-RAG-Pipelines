2. Why was the Taj Mahal built?

The Taj Mahal was commissioned by Shah Jahan in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631 while giving birth to their 14th child.

Shah Jahan ordered the construction of a magnificent mausoleum for her.

The Taj Mahal therefore functions primarily as a mausoleum — a monumental tomb.

Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan are both buried inside the monument.

3. When was it built?

Construction began around 1632, shortly after Mumtaz Mahal's death.

The main mausoleum was substantially completed around 1639–1643, while additional work on the surrounding complex continued for several more years.

The broader complex was completed around 1653.

Thousands of craftsmen and laborers participated in its construction.

4. Architecture

The Taj Mahal is famous for its perfect symmetry.

The central building has:

A huge white marble dome
Four tall minarets
Large arched entrances
Intricate marble decoration
Geometric designs
Calligraphy
A large surrounding garden

The four minarets are positioned around the central mausoleum.

A clever engineering feature is that the minarets are slightly angled outward, helping reduce the risk of them falling directly onto the main tomb in the event of structural failure.

5. The famous white marble

The Taj Mahal was primarily constructed using Makrana marble, quarried in Rajasthan.

The marble can appear to change color depending on the lighting:

🌅 Morning → soft pinkish tones
☀️ Daylight → bright white
🌙 Moonlight → bluish or silvery appearance

This changing appearance is one reason the Taj Mahal is so visually famous.

6. Marble decoration

The Taj Mahal isn't simply a plain white building.

Its marble contains incredibly detailed decorative work.

One famous technique is pietra dura, where colored stones are carefully cut and inlaid into the marble to create flowers, vines and geometric patterns.

Precious and semi-precious stones were used for some of these decorations.

The walls also contain elaborate Arabic calligraphy, particularly passages from the Quran.

7. The dome

The enormous central dome is one of the Taj Mahal's most recognizable features.

It rises approximately 73 metres above the ground.

The dome is topped by a decorative finial.

Four smaller domed structures surround the main dome, creating the distinctive silhouette seen from a distance.

8. The gardens

The Taj Mahal's garden follows the traditional charbagh concept.

The garden is divided into four main sections using pathways and water channels.

The long reflecting pool in front of the monument creates the famous photograph where the Taj Mahal appears reflected in the water.

The layout emphasizes symmetry and balance.

9. Symbolism

The Taj Mahal has come to represent:

❤️ Love — because of Shah Jahan's memorial to Mumtaz Mahal

⚰️ Remembrance — it is a mausoleum

🌿 Paradise — its garden and water design reflect Islamic ideas of paradise

⚖️ Balance and symmetry — almost every major element is carefully arranged around a central axis

10. The Taj Mahal today

The Taj Mahal became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

It is one of India's most visited monuments and is considered one of the finest examples of Mughal architecture.

It is also widely regarded as one of the world's most recognizable monuments.

In one sentence:

The Taj Mahal is a 17th-century Mughal mausoleum built by Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal, famous for its white marble, extraordinary symmetry, intricate decoration and enduring association with love.
